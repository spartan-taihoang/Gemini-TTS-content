Jesus spoke these words to his disciples on the last night before his crucifixion.
He knew they were about to face confusion... and fear.
And the image he gave them wasn't a battle plan.
It was a vine.
I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.
A vine isn't something you conquer, or master.
It's something you live in relationship with.
The branch doesn't produce fruit by straining, or willpower.
It produces fruit by staying connected... to the source of life.
And then Jesus makes the comparison direct.
I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.
Notice how that verse ends.
Without him... we can do nothing.
Not some things poorly.
Not most things with difficulty.
Nothing.
That's a hard word, in a world that celebrates self-reliance.
But it's also a deeply freeing one.
Because if the branch can't produce fruit on its own... then the branch isn't to blame when it struggles.
The question is never, am I working hard enough?
The question is... am I connected?
There's one word Jesus repeats in this passage more than any other.
Abide.
It's an old word, and it simply means to remain.
To stay.
To dwell.
Not to visit now and then, or check in when things get desperate.
But to live in a place... to make it your home.
Abide in me, and I in you.
As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me.
This isn't a technique.
It isn't a spiritual discipline to squeeze into an already crowded life.
It's about where you live.
It's about turning your whole self toward this one connection.
And Jesus says the Father is the gardener.
He prunes the branches, so they can bear more fruit.
Pruning can feel like loss.
But the gardener isn't punishing the branch.
He's caring for it... clearing away what hinders, so that life can flow more freely.
And tucked into this passage is a beautiful promise.
If ye abide in me, and my words abide in you, ye shall ask what ye will, and it shall be done unto you.
When you remain in him, and his words remain in you... your desires begin to line up with his.
You start to want what he wants.
And the asking and the receiving flow naturally... out of the relationship.
This isn't a formula for getting what you want.
It's what happens when you're so close to him... that his life becomes yours.
