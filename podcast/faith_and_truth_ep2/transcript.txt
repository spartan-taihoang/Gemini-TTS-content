There's an image in Scripture that has comforted believers for thousands of years.
You'll find it in Psalm one hundred nineteen... a long song of love for God's word.
Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.
A lamp unto my feet... and a light unto my path.
Think about that image for a moment.
In the ancient world, a lamp was a small clay vessel, filled with oil, with a wick that gave off a soft flame.
It didn't light up the whole road.
It didn't show you everything that lay ahead.
It showed you... the next step.
And that's exactly what the psalmist is telling us about God's word.
We often want Scripture to be a floodlight, don't we?
We want to open the Bible and find a detailed map of the next year... the next month... even the next week.
We want certainty.
We want to see the whole road, from here to there.
But that isn't what God promises.
He promises a lamp for the next step... a light for the path right in front of you.
And this matters, because faith isn't about knowing everything in advance.
Faith is trusting God... one step at a time.
Now, you might be wondering... how does Scripture actually do that?
How does it light the next step?
The apostle Paul wrote to a young pastor named Timothy, and explained what God's word is for.
All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: That the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works.
All Scripture, Paul says, is given by inspiration of God.
And it's profitable.
That word means useful... beneficial... valuable.
Profitable for doctrine... teaching us what is true.
For reproof... showing us what is not.
For correction... showing us how to get back on track when we've wandered.
And for instruction in righteousness... teaching us how to live in a way that pleases God.
Paul goes on... that the man of God may be perfect, thoroughly furnished unto all good works.
And perfect here means complete.
Mature.
Equipped for what God has called you to do.
So here's what this means.
Scripture doesn't necessarily tell you which job to take, or which house to buy, or whether to send that email tomorrow.
But it does something better.
It shapes the person who makes those decisions.
It teaches you who God is... faithful, holy, loving, just.
It teaches you who you are... made in his image, fallen, redeemed, loved.
It teaches you how God works... through grace, through patience, through ways that often surprise us.
And when you know those things... you have light for the next step.
Let me give you an example.
Suppose you're facing a choice tomorrow, and you're not sure what to do.
You open Scripture, and you don't find a verse that says, Do this specific thing.
But you read about God's character... about his faithfulness to those who trust him.
You read about his wisdom, and how he guides his people.
You read a command to love your neighbor... or to speak the truth... or to do your work as unto the Lord.
And suddenly... you have light.
You don't see the whole road.
But you see the next faithful step.
This is what it means for God's word to be a lamp.
It's not that Scripture answers every question we bring to it.
It's that Scripture equips us to walk faithfully... with the questions we carry.
And here's the other side of this truth.
If we're only looking for a floodlight, we might miss the lamp that's already shining.
We might overlook what God is showing us right now... because we're anxious about what we can't see further down the road.
But when we accept that God gives light for one step at a time... we're free to take that step.
Free to obey what we already know.
Free to trust that when we need the next piece of guidance... God will give it.
