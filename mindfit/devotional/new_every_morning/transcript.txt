Good morning. Before the day starts asking things of you, take a moment here. Today's devotional is called New Every Morning.
Perhaps you woke up rested. Or perhaps you woke up already behind... with yesterday's argument still in your head, or a list that began before your feet touched the floor. Either way, you are welcome here, just as you are.
Our verse comes from Lamentations, and that may seem a strange place to look for morning hope. It is a book of grief. Jerusalem had fallen. Homes were in ruins, people were hungry, and so much of what they loved was gone.
The writer doesn't hide any of it. He names his sorrow and his bitterness. And then, in the middle of all that loss, he deliberately turns his mind toward something he knows to be true.
It is of the Lord’s mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.
His compassions fail not. They are new every morning.
Notice what he does not say. He doesn't say the trouble is gone by morning. The city is still broken when the sun comes up. What is new is the mercy.
Long before, when God's people crossed the wilderness, He fed them with manna. They gathered enough for the day, and most days, they couldn't store it up for tomorrow. They had to trust that when they woke, there would be bread again. Mercy is a little like that. You don't have this whole year's strength this morning. You have today's.
So what did you carry over from yesterday? A mistake you keep replaying? A worry about someone you love? A quiet feeling that you should be further along by now?
You can be honest about it, as the writer was. And then you can set one true thing beside it. God's compassion did not run out last night. You are not starting today on what was left over. You are starting on mercy that is new.
And mercy received can become mercy given. Today, there may be someone who needs a fresh start from you. A softer answer. A second chance. You could be the place where they find something new this morning.
Let's pray. Faithful God, thank You for waking me into mercy I did not earn. I give You what I carried over from yesterday. Give me strength enough for today, and make me gentle with the people I meet. Great is Your faithfulness. Amen.
As you go, carry these few words with you... new every morning. Say them at the door, at your desk, or in the middle of a hard hour. Go gently into your day.
