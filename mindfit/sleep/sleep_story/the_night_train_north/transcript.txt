Good evening. Tonight's story is called The Night Train North. Before we begin, let your body settle... and let your shoulders drop away from your ears.
Take a slow breath in... and let it all the way out. You don't need to follow every word. Just let the story move along beside you, like the view from a window.
The station is old and made of red brick, with a tall clock that ticks loudly in the evening air. Snow has been falling since the afternoon, small and soft, and it gathers on the edges of the platform like sugar. A woman named Clara stands under the lamplight with a small suitcase and a ticket in her gloved hand.
The train comes in slowly, with a long, low sigh of steam. It is painted deep green, with gold letters on the side, and its windows glow a warm yellow. A conductor with a kind face and a gray mustache steps down and takes her ticket. Car number four, he says. The last cabin on the left.
The cabin is small, and perfect. There is a narrow bed made up with crisp white sheets and a thick wool blanket the color of oatmeal. There is a little folding table by the window, and a brass reading lamp with a green glass shade. Clara hangs up her coat, sits down on the edge of the bed, and lets out a long breath.
With a gentle jolt, the train begins to move. The platform slides away. The station lights grow smaller. And then the town is behind them, and there is only the dark, and the snow, and the steady sound of the wheels on the rails.
Clickety-clack. Clickety-clack. Clickety-clack.
There is a soft knock at the door. It is the conductor again, with a small tray. A pot of chamomile tea, a cup and saucer, and two shortbread biscuits wrapped in paper. For the journey, he says, and closes the door quietly behind him.
Clara pours the tea and holds the warm cup in both hands. Outside the window, the world is blue and white. Snowy fields stretch away in every direction, smooth and untouched. Now and then a farmhouse passes by, with one window lit, and smoke rising slowly from the chimney.
The train climbs gently into the hills. Pine trees appear, tall and dark, their branches heavy with snow. The moon comes out from behind a cloud, and suddenly everything shines silver. Clara turns off the reading lamp so she can see it better.
They pass a frozen lake, perfectly still, with the moon reflected in the middle of it like a coin. They cross a small stone bridge over a river that has not quite frozen, its dark water moving slowly under a lace of ice. They pass a village where every roof is white, and a church bell rings once, faint and far away.
Clickety-clack. Clickety-clack. The rhythm never changes. It is slow and steady, like a heartbeat, like breathing.
Somewhere in the night, the train stops at a tiny station in the middle of the forest. There is no one on the platform, just a single lantern and a wooden bench covered in snow. The train waits there for a minute, humming softly. Then, with another gentle sigh, it moves on.
Clara finishes her tea and sets the cup down on the little table. She slips under the wool blanket. The sheets are cool at first, and then warm. The pillow is soft and smells faintly of lavender. The train rocks her gently, from side to side, the way a boat rocks on calm water.
She thinks about where she is going, and then she lets the thought go. There is nothing to do until morning. No one needs anything from her. The train knows the way. All she has to do is rest.
And perhaps that is true for you tonight, too. The day is behind you, like the station lights. Tomorrow is still far down the track. Right now, there is only this quiet, steady journey through the dark, and the warmth around you.
Outside, the snow keeps falling, soft and slow. The moon follows the train across the sky. The pine trees pass by, one after another, after another. And the cabin is warm, and dim, and still.
Clickety-clack. Clickety-clack. Clickety... clack.
Let your breath slow down to match the rhythm. Breathe in... and breathe out. Let your body grow heavy, sinking into the bed, the way Clara sinks into hers. There is nowhere you need to be.
The train is carrying you north, through the snow, through the night. You don't need to stay awake for any of it. When you open your eyes, it will be morning. Rest now. Goodnight.
