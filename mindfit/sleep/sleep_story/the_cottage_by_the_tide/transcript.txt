Hello, and welcome. Tonight's story is called The Cottage by the Tide. Find a position that feels easy, and let the bed take all of your weight.
Breathe in slowly through your nose... and let it go. If your mind wanders while I speak, that's alright. Just come back to the sound of my voice, the way a wave comes back to the shore.
At the end of a long, sandy lane, where the grass gives way to dunes, there is a small white cottage with a blue door. It belongs to a man named Tom, who has lived by the sea for most of his life. He knows the sound of every kind of weather, and the smell of the air before rain.
It is early evening, at the very end of summer. The heat of the day has gone, and the air is soft and cool. The sun is sinking into the sea, slowly, the color of apricots. And the tide is going out, leaving the sand wet and shining.
Tom pulls on an old woolen sweater and steps outside. He walks barefoot down the little path through the dunes. The sand is still warm on top, and cool underneath. The tall grass whispers against his legs as he passes.
Down on the beach, the sea is calm. Small waves fold over, one after another, and slide up the sand in thin, lacy lines. They pause. And then they slip back again, with a soft hush, like someone saying, shh.
In... and out. In... and out. The sea has been breathing like this for longer than anyone can remember.
Tom walks slowly along the waterline. He isn't looking for anything in particular. Now and then he bends down to pick up a shell, smooth and pale pink inside. He turns it over in his fingers, and puts it in his pocket. A few gulls drift overhead, calling softly, heading home for the night.
Little rock pools have been left behind by the tide. Tom kneels beside one. The water is perfectly clear and perfectly still. A tiny crab sidles under a stone. A ribbon of green seaweed sways gently, back and forth, though there is no wind at all.
By the time he turns back toward the cottage, the sun has gone. The sky is fading from pink to lavender to deep, quiet blue. The first star appears just above the dunes. And in the cottage window, the lamp he left on is glowing, small and golden, waiting for him.
Inside, the cottage smells of wood and salt and old books. Tom lights a small fire in the stove, just enough to take the chill off. He fills the kettle, and while it heats, he sets the shell on the windowsill, beside all the others he has gathered over the years.
He makes a cup of warm milk with a little honey stirred in. Then he sits in his favorite armchair by the window, with a soft blanket over his knees. The fire crackles quietly. The kettle ticks as it cools. And outside, the sea keeps up its slow, steady rhythm.
Tom doesn't turn on the radio. He doesn't pick up a book. He simply sits, and listens, and lets the evening be enough. Some days are busy and full of noise. But this part of the day is his favorite... the quiet part, when everything has been done that needs doing.
The moon rises over the water, and lays a long, silver path across the sea, all the way to the shore. The waves catch the light as they fold over, and then they go dark again. Light... and dark. In... and out.
Let yourself be there with him now. The warm blanket. The low glow of the fire. The window, and the moon, and the sea just beyond it. Nothing to do. Nowhere to go.
After a while, Tom's eyes grow heavy. He finishes the last of the warm milk, banks the fire, and climbs the narrow stairs to his small bedroom under the roof. He leaves the window open just a crack, so he can hear the waves.
The bed is soft, and the quilt is heavy and warm. A cool breath of sea air drifts in through the window. And the sound of the tide fills the little room, gentle and endless.
Let your breathing follow the waves, the way Tom's does. Breathe in, as the wave rises... and breathe out, as it slides back down the sand. In... and out. Slower each time.
The tide will turn while you sleep. It will come back in, softly, all on its own, the way it always does. You don't need to watch it. Just rest. Goodnight.
