Hello again. Are you ready for a story? Tonight's story is about a girl named Kiko, a windy hill, and a kite that just would not fly.
Kiko lived in a little town with lots of hills. And every spring, all the children climbed the tallest hill to fly their kites. Red kites, yellow kites, kites shaped like fish and birds and dragons, all dancing in the sky.
This year, Kiko wanted to make her very own kite. So she found two thin sticks, some paper, a ball of string, and a long ribbon for the tail. She cut, and she folded, and she glued. And when she was finished, she held it up and smiled. It was perfect.
Kiko ran all the way to the top of the hill. She held the string tight, and she ran as fast as she could. The kite lifted up... up... and then, flop. It fell right down into the grass.
Kiko tried again. Flop. And again. Flop. Her cheeks were red, and her legs were tired. Some of the other children giggled. Kiko's eyes filled with tears. Maybe, she thought, I should just give up.
She sat down in the grass, holding her crumpled kite. Just then, an old man who was walking his dog stopped beside her. That's a fine kite, he said. It doesn't fly, said Kiko sadly.
The old man looked at it carefully. Hmm, he said. Every time it falls, it's telling you something. Let's listen. Maybe the tail is too short. Maybe the string is in the wrong place. Falling isn't failing. It's just learning.
So Kiko went home and tried again. She made the tail longer. She moved the string a little higher. The next day, she ran up the hill. The kite went higher this time... but then it wobbled, and fell. Kiko didn't cry. She thought, What is it telling me now?
She added a little more paper to one side, to make it even. She tied the knots tighter. Day after day, she tried, and fixed, and tried again.
And then, one bright, breezy morning, Kiko ran down the hill, let go of the kite... and it rose. Up past the trees. Up past the rooftops. Up, up, up, until it was a tiny speck dancing among the clouds, with its long tail waving hello.
Kiko laughed and laughed. And the children who had giggled before came running over. How did you do it? they asked. Kiko smiled. I just didn't stop trying, she said. Come on, I'll show you.
Kiko learned something important on that hill. When something is hard, it doesn't mean you can't do it. It just means you haven't done it yet. Every try, even the ones that fall, helps you get a little closer.
I wonder... is there something you're still learning to do? Remember Kiko, and keep trying.
That's the end of our story. Goodnight.
