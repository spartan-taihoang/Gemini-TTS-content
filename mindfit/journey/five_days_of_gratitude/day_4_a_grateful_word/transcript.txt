Welcome to Five Days of Gratitude. This is day four, A Grateful Word. We have been giving thanks to God. You are good. This is from You. Even here. Today, that thanks is going to find its way out of your mouth... and toward a person.
Let's get comfortable first. Soften your face, and the place between your eyes. Let your tongue rest. It does so much work. Take an easy breath... and for a moment, there is nothing you need to say to anyone.
So much of our gratitude never gets delivered. We feel it. We mean it. We think, I must tell her one day what that meant to me. And then the day fills up. Somewhere, there are people who changed our lives... who have no idea that they did.
Paul was in prison when he wrote to the church in Colossae. He couldn't visit them, or put a hand on anyone's shoulder. Words were what he had. And this is what he asked them to do with theirs, in chapter three, verse seventeen.
And whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God and the Father by him.
In word or deed. So thanks is not only a feeling that happens inside us. It has a voice, and it has hands. And when you thank a person for their kindness, you are not taking anything away from God. Very often... that person is how His kindness got to you.
Who comes to mind? It may be someone from this week, who did a small thing well. It may be someone from long ago. A teacher. A neighbor. A grandmother. The friend who didn't give advice. The stranger who was gentle on a terrible day. Let one face step forward... only one.
Now we'll build one sentence for them, in three pieces. First... when you. Name exactly what they did. Not, you're so nice. When you drove me home. When you remembered his name. Finish that piece in your heart.
The second piece... it helped me. Tell them what it did. It helped me feel less alone. It helped me keep going. And then the last piece, which needs nothing added to it at all... thank you. Put the three together now, and hear yourself say it to them.
If that person has died, or it wouldn't be wise or safe to contact them, your sentence is not wasted. Say it to God instead. He knows how to keep a thank you. And if you feel shy, that's all right. A grateful word can be clumsy, and still be one of the best things someone hears all year.
Here is our fourth sentence, for all the people His goodness has traveled through. Say it with the others. You are good. This is from You. Even here. Through them.
Let's pray. God and Father, thank You for the one whose face I have just seen. Thank You for loving me through their hands, and their time, and their words. Give me the courage to tell them. And make me, for somebody, a way that Your kindness arrives. In the name of the Lord Jesus. Amen.
Your practice for today is to deliver it. A message, a note, a call, or a word as you pass. Don't polish it. When you... it helped me... thank you. Send it before it has time to become one day. Go kindly.
