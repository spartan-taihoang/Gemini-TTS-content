Welcome to Five Days of Gratitude. This is day two, Daily Mercies. Our first sentence of thanks was, You are good. Today we add a second... and it begins with something very ordinary, close to where you are.
Take a moment to settle. Let whatever is holding you, hold you. Breathe in gently... and out. Now let your eyes or your hands find one plain thing nearby. A cup. A sleeve. A piece of bread. A pillow. Nothing special. Just keep it with you for a few minutes.
Most mercies don't arrive with a label on them. They look like groceries. They look like water from a tap, and a bus that came. We use them, and we wash them up, and we put them away. And because they come so often, we can start to think that they come from nowhere... or that they come only from us.
The book of Lamentations was written by someone who had watched nearly all the ordinary things disappear. The streets, the markets, the full tables of Jerusalem. And it was there, with so little left to point to, that he traced what remained back to its source. Chapter three, verses twenty-two and twenty-three.
It is of the Lord’s mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.
It is of the Lord's mercies. That little word, of, is doing something beautiful. It's the word you'd use for where a thing comes from. We are still here... and that comes from somewhere. It comes from Someone, whose compassion has not failed once.
So let's take your plain thing, and follow it home. Who put it there? Perhaps it was you. Perhaps someone who loves you. And before that, who sold it, or carried it, or drove it through the night? Who made it, with their tired and clever hands? You will never know their names. But see if you can picture one of them.
Now go further back. Before the hands, there was the stuff it's made of. Wheat, or cotton, or clay, or a tree. Before the wheat, there was soil. Before the soil could give anything, there was rain, and sun, and a seed that knew what to do in the dark. And before the seed... there was God.
Look at it again, your plain thing. It has traveled such a long way to reach you. Through fields, and weather, and strangers, and the kindness of God. It was never nothing. It was a mercy, all along, dressed in everyday clothes.
There may be things you need that haven't reached you yet. Following one mercy home doesn't deny that. It only reminds you who is at the other end of the road. The One who sent this... knows your address.
So here is our second sentence, beside the first. You are good. This is from You. Let's say them slowly, with your plain thing in view. You are good... This is from You.
Let's pray. Father, I have received so much without turning it over to see Your name. Thank You for this small thing, and every hand it passed through. Bless the people who made it and carried it. And open my eyes to the mercies I would have walked straight past. Great is Your faithfulness. Amen.
Before this day is over, follow one more thing home. Your supper, perhaps, or your shoes, or the light in the room. Go back as far as you can, until you reach Him. And then say it softly... this is from You.
