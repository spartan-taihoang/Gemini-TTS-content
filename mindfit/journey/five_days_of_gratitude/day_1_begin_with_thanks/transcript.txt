Welcome to Five Days of Gratitude. This is day one, Begin with Thanks. Over these five days, we'll learn five short sentences of thanks, one at a time. By the end, they will make one small prayer... that you can carry anywhere.
Let's start by arriving. Set down whatever is in your hands, if you can. Let your shoulders drop a little. Take a breath in... and let it go slowly. You don't have to feel thankful yet. You only have to be here.
When we hear the word gratitude, most of us reach for a list. My home, my family, my health. Those are good things to be thankful for. But lists can get shorter. Some days, the very thing at the top of the list is the thing that's in trouble. So we won't begin with the gifts. We'll begin with the Giver.
Psalm one hundred is a song of thanksgiving, for people on their way in to worship. You can almost hear their feet on the road, and their voices getting louder as they near the gates. These are verses four and five.
Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name. For the Lord is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations.
Did you notice the reason? It doesn't say, for the harvest was good. It doesn't say, for the year went well. It says... for the Lord is good. That was true before today began. And it will still be true, whatever today turns out to be.
So picture a gate in front of you. An old one, wooden, worn pale where many hands have pushed it open. People have walked through it laughing. People have walked through it limping. No one is stopped, and asked how their week has been. There is only one thing to bring with you as you step through... and that is thanks.
Before you step, choose one word for who God is. Not something He has given you. Something He is. Kind. Patient. Faithful. Near. It might be the word you've known to be true for years. Or it might be the one you most need to be true today. Take a moment, and choose yours.
Now, hold that word, and walk slowly through the gate. As you breathe in, say it silently to Him. You are... and then your word. As you breathe out... thank You. Again. You are... Thank You. Once more, in your own time.
If your word felt a little hollow just now, that's all right. Thanks doesn't have to be felt all the way down before it counts. Sometimes we say what is true, and the heart follows a few steps behind. It's still coming through the gate.
And here is the first of our five sentences. It's the simplest, and all the others will stand on it. You are good. Say it with me, very quietly. You are good.
Let's pray. Lord, I come in through Your gate with the little thanks I have. Before You give me anything else today, You are already good. Your mercy doesn't run out when my feelings do. Teach me to begin here... with You. Amen.
For the rest of this day, choose one doorway you often walk through. A kitchen, a car door, the front step. When you pass through it, let it be your gate, and say those three words. You are good. That's all day one asks of you. Go gently.
