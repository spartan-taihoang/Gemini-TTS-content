Welcome to Five Days of Gratitude. This is day three, Thanks in All Things. So far we have two sentences. You are good... and, This is from You. Today's is the shortest, and it may be the hardest. We'll take it slowly.
Find a position that's kind to your body. If something hurts, you don't need to hide it for this. Let your breath come as it comes. And rest your two hands somewhere you can feel them... on your knees, on a blanket, or simply at your sides.
There's a kind of gratitude that can be cruel. It tells a person in pain to count their blessings. It says, at least. It says, others have it worse. If anyone has ever handed you thankfulness like that, I'm sorry. That is not what God is asking of you today.
Paul wrote to a young church in Thessalonica that was suffering for its faith. They had real reasons to be afraid, and real losses to mourn. He didn't tell them that those things were good. What he wrote is in his first letter, chapter five, verse eighteen.
In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.
Listen to the first word. In. In the waiting room. In the argument that isn't mended. In the month with too little money. You are not asked to give thanks for the illness, or the loss, or the harm that someone did to you. Only that thanks may be allowed in the room with it.
And giving thanks never means staying where you are being hurt. If you are not safe, or it's all too heavy to carry, you can be grateful to God and still ask for help, right away. Reaching for help can be part of His will for you, too.
Now, your hands. Let one of them close gently, not tight, around the hard thing. You don't have to solve it, or describe it nicely. Just name it to God. Lord, this is hard. Let that hand stay closed for now. He is not going to pry it open.
And let the other hand turn over, and open. It doesn't have to let go of anything, because it isn't holding the hard thing. It is only free to receive. Is there one thing that could rest there? A person who checked on you. An hour of sleep. The strength that got you as far as this minute. Or only this... that He has not left.
Feel both hands at once. One closed. One open. Neither is lying. The hard thing is true, and the gift is true, and both of these hands belong to you. This is what thanks in all things can look like. It doesn't take two open hands. One is enough.
So here is our third sentence, two words long. Let's say it after the others. You are good. This is from You. Even here. If that's as much as you can say today, say only the last one. Even here.
Let's pray. Lord Jesus, You gave thanks and broke the bread on the night You were betrayed. So You know how to hold sorrow and thanks together. Take hold of the hand I cannot open. Fill the one I can. And where I have no thanks at all, stay with me until I do. Amen.
When you meet the hard thing again today, you don't need a speech. Just turn one hand over, wherever you are. Under a table, in a pocket, on the wheel. One open hand... and two small words. Even here.
