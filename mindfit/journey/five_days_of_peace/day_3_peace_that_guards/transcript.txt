Welcome to Five Days of Peace. This is day three, Peace That Guards. So far we have two steps. Palms up, here it is. Shoulders down, it's Yours. Today's step turns worry around, until it becomes a conversation.
Come as you are. If your mind is busy, it can be busy in here. Let your feet or your back find something solid. And for one breath, pay attention only to the nearest thing to you... the cloth on your skin, the air by your face. Something is already very close.
Have you noticed whom we talk to when we worry? Usually, it's ourselves. We talk about the problem. We talk about the people in it. We hold whole meetings about tomorrow, and God is the subject of some of them. But being talked about... is very different from being talked to.
Paul was a prisoner when he wrote to his friends in Philippi. Just before his famous words about worry, he slips in one short sentence. The Lord is at hand. At hand... near enough to touch. Everything that follows rests on that. It is in chapter four, verses six and seven.
Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.
To be careful, in older English, was to be full of care. So this is no scolding for anxious people. It is more like a friend touching your arm while you mutter, and saying... He's right here. You can say that to Him.
Let's try it with one worry. First, say it the usual way, about. What if I can't manage this? Now turn, and say it to. Lord, will You help me manage this? Do you feel the small difference? The trouble is the same size. But there are two of you in the room.
Now use your own. Take the sentence that has been circling, and begin it with His name. Lord... and then ask for exactly what you need. Help me sleep. Help me wait. Help me make the call. Make it as small and as honest as you like.
And before you leave that request with Him, add two words that look backward. Thank You. For being at hand. For one time He has helped you before. For hearing this, before you know how He will answer. You don't need a long list. One true thank You will do.
Then Paul makes a promise. Peace will keep you. He doesn't say that you will keep the peace, gripping it tightly all day. It does the guarding. It can stand watch over a mind that still has questions... and over a heart that understands very little of what is going on.
Here is our third step, with the others. Palms up... here it is. Shoulders down... it's Yours. And now, a hand resting on your heart, where His peace keeps watch... help me, and thank You. If you never learn another step, these three are a whole prayer. Here it is. It's Yours. Help me, and thank You.
Let's pray. Lord Jesus, You are nearer than the thing I fear. I have talked about it long enough, so now I am talking to You. Help me, in the very place I named. Thank You for hearing. And set Your peace around my heart and mind, until I understand, and even if I don't. Amen.
Whenever you catch yourself talking about the worry today, turn one sentence around. Put His name at the front, and thanks at the end. You have what you need for this, and you can return to these three steps as often as you like. Go in peace... He is at hand.
