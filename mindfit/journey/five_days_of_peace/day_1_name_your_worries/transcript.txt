Welcome to Five Days of Peace. This is day one, Name Your Worries. Over these five days, we'll learn five small steps, each with a few words to say. Together they'll become a way back to peace... one you can use in less than a minute, wherever worry finds you.
For now, just arrive. Let whatever is beneath you take your weight. Let your breath move at its own speed. You don't have to calm down before we start. Worried is a perfectly good way to come to God.
Worry often behaves like a message we can't stop rereading. We fold it up and put it away. A few minutes later, we take it out again. We study every line for what it might mean. And the more privately we read it, the larger the words become.
A king named Hezekiah once received a letter like that. A great army had swept through his country, and now it threatened Jerusalem. The letter said, in effect, don't imagine that your God can save you. Every frightening sentence was written down, in ink. And here is what he did with it, in Second Kings, chapter nineteen, verse fourteen.
And Hezekiah received the letter of the hand of the messengers, and read it: and Hezekiah went up into the house of the Lord, and spread it before the Lord.
Did you see what he did? He didn't tear it up. He didn't pretend it was good news. He carried the whole thing into God's presence, and he smoothed it out flat... as if to say, You read it, Lord. Read what I have been reading.
So if your worry were a letter, what would it say? Give it a first line. Dear me... you are going to lose your job. Or, the results will be bad. Or, they are never coming back. Let it use its real words, short and plain. There's no need to make them sound faithful.
Now picture a wide wooden table, and God on the other side of it. Take the letter out. Unfold it, one crease at a time. Smooth down the corners with your hands. Then turn it around, so that the words are facing Him... and you don't have to be the only one reading them.
If any line in your letter describes real danger, spreading it before God can include showing it to someone who can help. And if worry ever tells you to harm yourself, please don't stay alone with those words. Call your local emergency number, or a crisis line, or a person who can come to you. Hezekiah sent for help, too.
Here is the first of our five steps. If you can, turn your hands over, palms up, like a page laid open. And say these three words to God. Here it is. Once more, with your palms open. Here it is.
Let's pray. Lord, You know how to read. Read what frightens me. Read the lines I have added myself, and the lines that are true. I spread all of it out before You, and I won't hide the worst sentence. Bow down Your ear, and hear me. Amen.
When worry brings out its letter again today, don't read it by yourself. Turn your palms up, wherever they are, and show Him the page. Here it is. That is all day one asks of you. Go gently.
