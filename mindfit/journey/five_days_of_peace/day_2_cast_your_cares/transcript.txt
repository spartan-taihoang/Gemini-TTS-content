Welcome to Five Days of Peace. This is day two, Cast Your Cares. Our first step was to turn our palms up and show God the worry, with three words... here it is. Today we learn what can happen next.
Let's settle. Notice your shoulders, and how high they've climbed. You don't need to pull them down. Just notice them, the way you might notice someone standing in a doorway with their coat still on. Breathe in easily... and breathe out.
There is a kind of worry that we don't hold in our hands at all. We wear it. It goes on in the morning with our clothes. It gets heavy, like wool in the rain. And after a while we stop feeling it as a thing we put on... and begin to think it is simply who we are.
The apostle Peter wrote to believers who had plenty of reasons to be worried. They were scattered, misunderstood, and suffering for their faith. He doesn't tell them their cares are imaginary. He tells them what to do with them, and why, in his first letter, chapter five, verse seven.
Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.
That word for casting turns up in one other story. When Jesus rode toward Jerusalem, His friends took off their cloaks and threw them over the back of a young donkey. It's the same word. They didn't hold on to a sleeve. The cloak left their shoulders... and something else carried it up the road.
But listen to the reason Peter gives. It isn't, because worry is wrong. It isn't, because you ought to be stronger. It is because He cares for you. Before you are someone with a problem, you are someone He is fond of. The coat matters to Him, because of who has been shivering inside it.
So let's take it off. Think of one care that you have been wearing. Feel where it sits... across the neck, down the back. Now, in your imagination, undo the top button. And the next. Slip one arm free, and then the other. Feel the air where the weight was. And lay the whole heavy thing across His arms.
What is it like to stand there without it, even for a breath? Perhaps it's lighter. Perhaps you feel bare, and you want it back. Either one is all right. Let Him look at you for a moment... not at the coat. At you.
You may find it on your shoulders again by supper time. That isn't weak faith. It's a long habit, and you can take it off as often as you notice. And giving God your care never means giving up your medicine, or your treatment, or the people helping you. Those may be some of the ways that He is caring.
Here is our second step, after the first. Palms up... here it is. Now let your shoulders drop, just a little, as if a coat were sliding from them... and say, it's Yours. Both together, slowly. Here it is. It's Yours.
Let's pray. Father, I have worn this for so long. I take it off in front of You, and I ask You to carry what I was never made to wear. Thank You that You care about the care... and that You care far more for me. Teach my shoulders to believe it. Amen.
For the rest of today, let every coat you see remind you. One on a hook, one over a chair, one on a stranger at the crossing. Drop your shoulders, and say it under your breath... it's Yours. Go lightly.
