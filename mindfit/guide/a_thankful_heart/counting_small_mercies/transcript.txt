Welcome to A Thankful Heart. This practice is called Counting Small Mercies. You don't need a wonderful day for this. An ordinary one will do... even one that has only just begun.
Let yourself settle where you are. Loosen your shoulders, and take an easy breath. There is nothing to produce in these few minutes, and no list you have to finish. We are only going to look slowly at what is already here.
Small mercies are easy to miss, because they behave so well. The tap runs, and we don't think about water. The light comes on. A knee that hurt last month carries us up the stairs. A trouble gets our attention by knocking. A mercy often lets itself in quietly... and puts the kettle on.
Perhaps that is why David, in Psalm one hundred three, has to talk to his own soul. He doesn't tell it to go and find new blessings. He tells it to remember the ones it has. These are his words, from verse two.
Bless the Lord, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits.
Forget not. It is such a gentle instruction. It assumes the gifts have been given. And it knows how quickly a busy heart forgets them. So let's do a little remembering, together with God.
Begin as close as your own body. One breath, arriving without being asked. Thank You. Your eyes, or your ears, or the hands that have done so much for you. Choose one. Thank You. Whatever is holding your weight right now. Thank You.
Now widen the circle to the room. Something that keeps you warm, or dry, or safe. Thank You. Something you use each day and almost never notice... a spoon, a key, a pair of worn shoes. Thank You. Something in view that is lovely for no useful reason at all. Thank You.
And now, a person. Someone who made your life a little lighter, perhaps without knowing it. They held a door. They answered. They stayed. Let their face come to mind for a moment... and instead of hurrying on, say their name to God. Thank You, Lord, for them.
Is there a mercy that would have been an answered prayer, some years ago? Something you once longed for, that has since become ordinary? A home, a friend, a skill, a little more peace than you used to have. See if you can meet it again, as if it had only just arrived.
You may have found only one thing. One is not too few. Thankfulness isn't measured by the length of the list. It begins whenever a gift leads your eyes back to the Giver... and one spoon, truly noticed, can do that.
Let's pray. Generous God, forgive me for walking past so much kindness. Thank You for the mercies I counted, and the many more I will never see. Slow my eyes. Soften my heart. And help my soul to forget not all that You have done. Amen.
As you go back to your day, let one ordinary thing interrupt you. Water on your hands. A light in a window. When it does, you only need two words... thank You. Go gently, and keep noticing.
