Welcome to A Thankful Heart. This practice is called Thanks in Hard Seasons. If giving thanks feels difficult right now, you don't have to pretend otherwise. You are welcome here, exactly as it is.
Let your body be held by whatever is under you. You don't need to lift your face, or fix your expression. Breathe in as gently as you can... and as you breathe out, let yourself stop being brave for a few minutes.
Sometimes thankfulness has been handed to us like a correction. Look on the bright side. Others have it worse. But the Bible never asks you to call a painful thing good. Its people cry out, and question, and weep... and some of them learn to give thanks with the tears still on their faces.
The prophet Habakkuk was one of them. He begins by asking God, how long must I cry for help? By the end of his book, he can see disaster coming, and his whole body trembles. And then he prays this, in chapter three, verses seventeen and eighteen.
Although the fig tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls: Yet I will rejoice in the Lord, I will joy in the God of my salvation.
He names every empty place. The tree. The vine. The field. The fold. The stall. He doesn't skip one of them to reach the joy sooner. And then comes one small word, that carries so much weight. Yet.
So what is your although? Although the answer hasn't come. Although the chair is empty, or the money is short, or the body is not healing as you hoped. Say your own sentence quietly to God, beginning with that word. Although...
Let that sentence stand, just as you said it. He heard it. You don't need to soften it, or explain it, or apologize for it. An honest although is already a prayer... and it is already held.
Now, only if you are able, look for one thing beside it. Not instead of it. Beside it. A person who has stayed. A meal you managed to eat. An hour when the pain loosened a little. Or simply this... that you are still here, and God is still God. Place it softly after your although. Yet... I thank You for this.
And if you can't find a yet today, that is all right. Habakkuk had a whole book of questions before he reached his. Until yours comes, you can borrow his. God is not waiting for your thanks before He will stay close to you.
In winter, an orchard can look as if it has nothing to give. Bare branches, hard ground, no color anywhere. No one would ask it for fruit. It is enough, in that season, for the roots to hold. Your thanks may be like a root just now... small, and hidden, and holding on.
Let's pray. God of my salvation, You see every empty place I have named. I will not call them good. But I will call You good, as well as I am able. Receive the small thanks I have, and carry me where I have none. And where I need help, send me people to help me. Amen.
Take one word with you into the rest of this season. You don't have to say it loudly. It can be a whisper, with everything still unfinished... yet. Go gently. You are not walking through it alone.
