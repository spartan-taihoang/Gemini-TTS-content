Welcome to Grief and Comfort. This practice is called Permission to Grieve. I'm so sorry for what you have lost. You don't have to be strong in here. You don't have to be anything at all.
Let your body be as heavy as it is. If tears come, let them come. And if none come, that is all right too. Grief can be loud, and it can be completely numb. Both are grief... and both are welcome.
After a loss, people often mean to be kind, and they hand us reasons to stop crying. At least it was quick. At least you had those years. Everything happens for a reason. And we nod, and we thank them... and we learn to do our weeping in the car, or in the shower, where it won't trouble anyone.
There is a story in the Gospel of John about a family in Bethany. Two sisters, Martha and Mary, and their brother Lazarus, whom Jesus loved. Lazarus fell ill, and he died. When Jesus arrived, each sister said the same thing to Him. Lord, if You had been here, my brother would not have died.
He didn't correct Mary. He didn't tell her to dry her eyes. He saw her weeping, and the friends weeping with her, and He was deeply troubled. He asked where they had laid him, and they said... come and see. And then we are given this, in John chapter eleven, verse thirty-five.
Jesus wept.
He knew what He was about to do. He knew that in a little while, He would call His friend out of the grave. And still, He cried. Knowing the end of the story did not make the sorrow foolish. So your hope of heaven doesn't have to cancel your tears. You can believe with all your heart... and still stand at a grave and weep.
The people who were watching didn't say, look how weak His faith is. They said, behold how He loved him. That is what your grief is. It is love, with the one it was given to out of reach. There is nothing in it that you need to apologize for.
So what have you not given yourself permission to feel? Sadness, long after others expected it to pass? Anger? Relief, after a long illness? Laughter, that arrived at the wrong moment? Or nothing... just a great, gray tiredness? Choose whichever is truest, and let it have a place to sit.
I'll say some words slowly, and you can receive the ones you need. You may cry. You may not cry. You may miss them for as long as you live. You may say their name out loud. You may have a good hour, without feeling guilty. You may have a terrible one, without explaining why. You are allowed to grieve.
Let's pray. Lord Jesus, who wept beside a grave, thank You that I don't need to hide my sorrow from You. You know the name I am carrying. You know what went with them when they left. Where I have scolded myself for hurting, teach me to be gentle. And when I have no words, let my tears be the prayer. Amen.
There is nothing you need to finish before you go. Take your grief with you, just as it is. It is allowed to come along... and so is the One who wept.
