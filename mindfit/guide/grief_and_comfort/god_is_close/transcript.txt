Welcome to Grief and Comfort. This practice is called God Is Close. There's very little for you to do here. Mostly, we are going to let you be held... and pray the words you may be too tired to find.
Lean back into whatever can take your weight. A cushion, a wall, the seat beneath you. If it helps, wrap something around your shoulders. Let your breath be small, if small is all there is. You don't have to breathe deeply to be loved.
Loss changes the size of a house. The rooms grow larger, and much too quiet. You reach for the phone, to tell them something, and then you remember. And in that remembering, you can feel more alone than you knew a person could be. Even God can seem to have stepped out of the room.
There is a psalm of David that speaks straight into that room. It doesn't say that God waits for us to recover, and then draws near. It tells us where He already is. It is Psalm thirty-four, verse eighteen.
The Lord is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.
Nigh. It's an old word for near. Near, as in within reach. Near, as in close enough to hear what is said under the breath. And near to whom? To the broken heart. The very thing that makes you feel far away from Him... is the place He has chosen to be.
Think of someone sitting up with a person they love, through a long and painful night. They don't talk much. They don't ask to be entertained, or thanked. They pull the chair a little closer, and they stay. If the one in the bed falls asleep, they stay. If the one in the bed turns away, and faces the wall... still, they stay.
Let God be that close to you now. You don't have to turn toward Him. Just allow the chair to be there. As you breathe in... the Lord is near. As you breathe out... to my broken heart. Again, slowly. The Lord is near... to my broken heart. And once more, without the words... only the breath, and the nearness.
I'll pray for you now, and you can simply listen. Father, be near to this child of Yours. Be near when they first wake, and it all comes back. Be near at the table, where a place is empty. Be near on the birthdays, and the anniversaries, and the ordinary afternoons that hurt for no reason anyone else can see. Be near when the visitors stop coming, and the cards stop arriving. When Your child cannot hold on to You, hold on to Your child.
And Father, for the one they miss... You know that life completely, every year and every hour of it. We place that dear name into Your hands, where love is not lost. Keep what we cannot keep. And give Your comfort, a little at a time, as it can be borne. In the name of Jesus. Amen.
If you felt nothing during that prayer, it was still prayed. He is still close. His nearness doesn't depend on your noticing it. And you don't have to make do with only an unseen comfort. Let someone sit in the real chair, too. A friend, a pastor, a grief counselor, anyone who can stay a while... and let you be sad.
May the Lord be near you in the next hour. May He be near you in the one after that. And in every room that has grown too quiet... may you find that you are not alone.
