Welcome to Healing and Hope. This practice is called Honest Lament. Here, you don't need careful words, or a brave face. You can tell God the truth.
Find a position your body can bear for a few minutes. Unclench your hands, if they will open. If they won't, let them stay as they are. Take one breath that doesn't have to be calm. It only has to be yours.
Many of us were taught to tidy our prayers before we bring them. To say, I'm fine, and I trust You... and nothing in between. But the Bible is full of untidy prayers. They are called laments. A lament is pain that has turned its face toward God. It is not a failure of faith. It is faith, refusing to stop talking to Him.
David wrote one that is only six verses long. Four times in its first two verses, he asks the same question... how long? Listen to how it begins, in Psalm thirteen, verse one.
How long wilt thou forget me, O Lord? for ever? how long wilt thou hide thy face from me?
He says that to God. To His face. No apology, and no softening. And God did not strike those words from the book. He kept them... so that you would have them, on a day like this.
So what is your how long? How long will this hurt? How long until the phone rings, or the body mends, or the house stops feeling so empty? Say it to Him now, in your own plain words. Begin with... how long, O Lord.
And if there is more, you can say that too. Lord, this is what I have lost. Lord, this is what I don't understand. Lord, this is what I am angry about. Lord, this is what I need. You don't have to use every line. One true sentence is a whole prayer.
Near the end of his psalm, David's questions are still unanswered. Nothing around him has changed, that we are told of. And yet, beside the questions, he sets down one more true thing. It is in verse five.
But I have trusted in thy mercy; my heart shall rejoice in thy salvation.
You may not be ready for that verse today. You don't have to hurry to it. God can hold your question for as long as it takes. And if the pain ever tells you that you would be better off gone, or that you might harm yourself, please don't stay alone with it. Call your local emergency number, or a crisis line, or someone who can be with you now. That, too, is an honest cry for help.
Let's pray. Lord, You heard what I said, and what I couldn't say. I have not hidden it from You. Do not hide Your face from me. Stay in the question with me... and when I am able, teach my heart to trust Your mercy again. Amen.
You told the truth, and you are still loved. There's nothing you need to take back. Leave the words with Him... and go as softly as you need to.
