Welcome to Finding Rest. This practice is called Praying the Psalms. If you have no words left at the end of the day, that's all right. Tonight, we can borrow some.
Get comfortable, in whatever way is kindest to your body. Let your breath come and go. You don't need to sound faithful, or feel peaceful, before you pray. You can come exactly as tired as you are.
The Psalms are prayers that people have shared for thousands of years. They are full of thanks and tears, questions and songs. When our own words run out, we can step inside theirs, and find that there is room for us.
Psalm four is a prayer for evening. The one praying it has known distress. People around them are asking, who will show us any good? And still, the prayer ends in bed, with these words from verse eight.
I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, Lord, only makest me dwell in safety.
We are going to pray that verse one small piece at a time... the way you might hold a smooth stone, and turn it over slowly in your hand. I'll say a piece, and you can repeat it silently. Then we'll make it your own.
I will both lay me down... I will both lay me down. Lord, here is my body. My heavy legs, my aching back, my busy hands. I have carried myself all day. Now I lay myself down before You.
In peace... in peace. Lord, not everything around me is settled. You know the conversation that still hurts, and the answer I am still waiting for. I don't have to fix them in order to receive Your peace. Meet me in the middle of them.
And sleep... and sleep. Lord, I can't make myself sleep. I can only stop, and let it come. So I loosen my hold on this night. Whether sleep comes quickly or slowly, I am resting with You.
For thou, Lord, only... thou, Lord, only. Not my planning. Not my watching, or my worrying, or getting everything right. You alone are God, and I am glad that I am not.
Makest me dwell in safety... dwell in safety. Lord, let me live inside Your care, and not just visit it. Be near to this room, and to the people I love who are sleeping somewhere else tonight. I trust them to You as well.
Now hear the whole verse again, as your own prayer. Let each word rest where it lands. I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, Lord, only makest me dwell in safety.
Lord, thank You for giving me words when I had none. Hear what I said, and what I didn't know how to say. Let Your word be the last voice I listen to today. Amen.
If you are awake a little longer, choose the piece that felt most like yours. Perhaps it is only two words... in peace. Say it slowly, as often as you like. You don't have to reach the end of it. Goodnight.
