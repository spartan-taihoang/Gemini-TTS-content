Welcome to Calming an Anxious Heart. This practice is called Name the Worry. If your thoughts are loud right now, you don't have to quiet them before you begin. You can come with all of them.
Wherever you are, let yourself be supported. Feel your feet, or your back, or the weight of your hands. Look gently around you, and notice one thing that is simply still. A wall... a cup... the corner of a window. Let your breath out, without hurrying it.
Worry rarely arrives one thought at a time. A bill becomes the whole month. A short message becomes a broken friendship. A small pain becomes the worst news you can imagine. Soon it is hard to say what is wrong... only that everything feels wrong.
The Bible makes room for a mind like that. In Psalm ninety-four, someone who feels their foot slipping tells God the truth about what is happening inside them. These are their words, from verse nineteen.
In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul.
The multitude of my thoughts. A crowd of them. And notice where God's comfort arrives. It doesn't wait outside until the crowd has gone home. It comes right into the middle of it.
So let's look at the crowd kindly, and find just one face in it. What is the worry that is nearest to you right now? It doesn't have to be the biggest, or the most sensible. Only the one that is here.
Try putting it into a few plain words. I am worried that... and let yourself finish the sentence silently. You don't need to explain it, or defend it. Say it once more, a little more slowly. I am worried that...
Now notice where that worry lives in your body. Perhaps in your chest. Your throat. Your stomach. Your shoulders. You don't have to make it leave. Rest a hand there if you like, as you might with someone you love... and let that place be cared for.
Next, gently separate what you know from what you fear. There may be something true here, something that does need your care. And there may be a story your mind has added, about how it all ends. You can say to yourself... this part I know. And this part, I don't know yet.
Not knowing yet is uncomfortable. But it is different from knowing the worst. And there is no part of it, the known or the unknown, where God is absent. You have named one worry in His presence. You are not alone with it now.
Hear the verse once more, and put your own worry inside it. In the multitude of my thoughts within me... even this thought... thy comforts delight my soul.
Let's pray. God of all comfort, You know the worry I have just named, and the ones I haven't found words for. Show me what is mine to care for today. Stay with me in what I cannot know yet. And if this is too heavy to carry alone, give me courage to tell someone who can help. Amen.
When the crowd gathers again, you don't need to answer every voice. Find one worry, and call it by its name. Then remember who is standing there with you. Go gently.
