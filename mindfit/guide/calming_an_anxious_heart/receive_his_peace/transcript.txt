Welcome to Calming an Anxious Heart. This practice is called Receive His Peace. You don't have to manufacture calm in these few minutes. We are simply going to ask, give thanks, and make room to receive.
Let yourself arrive where you are. Notice the air on your skin, and the place where your body meets its support. Let your face rest. Breathe in gently... and breathe out, with nothing to prove.
When we are anxious, we often bargain with peace. I'll rest when the results come back. I'll relax when they call. I'll be all right when this is over. And so peace keeps moving further down the road... always just past the next answer.
Paul knew about waiting for answers. He was a prisoner when he wrote to his friends in Philippi, and he didn't know how his case would end. Yet he offers them a peace that can begin before the answer comes. We read it in Philippians, chapter four, verses six and seven.
Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.
To be careful, in that older English, means to be full of care. To be anxious. Paul isn't scolding anyone for feeling that way. He is showing us where the care can go. Into prayer. Into asking. Into thanks.
So begin by asking. You can be very simple and very specific. Lord, please help me with... and name what you need. You don't have to choose careful words. A request from a tired heart is still a request, and He hears it.
Now, beside that request, set three small thanks. One thing your senses can enjoy... a taste, a color, a warm place to sit. Thank You. One person who has been kind to you, even briefly. Thank You. One hard day, behind you now, that God has already brought you through. Thank You.
Thanksgiving doesn't pretend the trouble is good. It remembers that the trouble isn't all there is. Your need is real. And these gifts are real. You can hold them both before God, without choosing between them.
And then comes the promise. The peace of God shall keep your hearts and minds. The word keep is a guarding word, like a watch standing at a city gate. You don't have to guard the peace. His peace guards you.
So picture that gate, at the edge of your heart. You have been standing there a long while, checking everything that approaches. Now someone stronger has come to take the watch. You can step back from the gate. You can sit down. Breathe in, and receive... breathe out, and be kept.
This peace passes understanding, so it may not make sense yet. Nothing may have been solved in the last few minutes. You can be unfinished, and still be held. You can be waiting, and still be at peace.
Let's pray. Lord Jesus, thank You for hearing what I asked, and for every gift I named. I receive the peace I cannot explain. Stand watch over my heart where it aches, and over my mind where it circles. Keep me, while I wait. Amen.
As you return to what is next, you can leave the gate to Him. When worry knocks, turn it into one honest request, and one small thanks. And let His peace answer the door. Go in peace.
