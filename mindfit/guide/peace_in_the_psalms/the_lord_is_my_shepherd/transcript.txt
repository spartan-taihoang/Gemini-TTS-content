Welcome to Peace in the Psalms. This reading is called The Lord Is My Shepherd. We'll listen slowly to Psalm twenty-three, spend a little while with it, and then pray. You don't need to study anything. You can simply listen.
Let yourself be comfortable. Set down whatever was in your hands. If your mind is moving quickly, there's no need to catch it. Just give it something gentle to walk beside... one old song, one line at a time.
A restless mind is often a counting mind. It counts what is missing, and what might run out. Not enough time. Not enough money. Not enough of me to go around. Listen for how this psalm answers all that counting, in its very first line.
The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want. He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters. He restoreth my soul: he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name’s sake.
Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me.
Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies: thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over. Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life: and I will dwell in the house of the Lord for ever.
I shall not want. It doesn't say I will never feel need. There is hunger in this psalm, and there are shadows, and enemies. But there is no moment in it without a shepherd. I shall not want... because I am not the one who has to provide it all.
And notice how soon the psalm lets us lie down. It comes near the beginning, before the valley has been crossed, before the table is set. Rest isn't the prize at the end of this journey. It is one of the first things the Shepherd gives.
So what has your mind been counting? What are you afraid there will not be enough of? Name it to yourself, without any shame. And then set the first line right beside it. The Lord is my shepherd... even here. I shall not want.
Let a few of the words rest on you now. Green pastures... and let your shoulders lower. Still waters... and let your breath slow. He restoreth my soul... and let yourself be someone who is being restored, rather than someone who must repair everything.
If green pastures feel very far from where you are, hold on to the middle of the psalm. For thou art with me. Those five words do not need good weather. They are as true in the valley as they are beside the water.
Let's pray. Lord, my Shepherd, You know what I lack, and what I only fear I will lack. Quiet the counting in me. Lead me to the rest You have already made, and walk close when the path grows dark. Let goodness and mercy find me today. Amen.
Take the first line with you. When the counting begins again, you can answer it softly... the Lord is my shepherd. I shall not want. Go gently, and be led.
