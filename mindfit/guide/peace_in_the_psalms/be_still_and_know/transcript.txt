Welcome to Peace in the Psalms. This reading is called Be Still and Know. We'll listen to the whole of Psalm forty-six. It is a loud psalm in places... and it has a great stillness near its end.
Find somewhere your body can be supported. Feel what is steady beneath you. You may have come here with a great deal of noise... from the news, from your home, from inside your own head. Bring the noise along. This psalm knows what to do with it.
As you listen, you will hear the word Selah. No one is certain what it means, but many understand it as a place to pause. So when you hear it, you don't need to do anything. Let it be permission to stop for a breath.
God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea; Though the waters thereof roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with the swelling thereof. Selah.
There is a river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacles of the most High. God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved: God shall help her, and that right early. The heathen raged, the kingdoms were moved: he uttered his voice, the earth melted. The Lord of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge. Selah.
Come, behold the works of the Lord, what desolations he hath made in the earth. He maketh wars to cease unto the end of the earth; he breaketh the bow, and cutteth the spear in sunder; he burneth the chariot in the fire. Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth. The Lord of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge. Selah.
Mountains falling. Waters roaring. Kingdoms moved. The psalm doesn't ask us to pretend the world is calm. It looks straight at everything that shakes... and then it tells us what does not.
In the middle of all that thundering water, there is another kind of water. A river, with streams that make a city glad. It doesn't roar. It simply keeps arriving. God's help can be like that... quieter than the trouble, and still flowing when the storm has tired itself out.
She shall not be moved, the psalm says of that city. And the reason is not the strength of her walls. God is in the midst of her. You don't have to be unshakable. You have Someone in the midst of you who is.
And then, be still. The old word behind it can mean let go... let your hands drop. So let them drop now. Be still, and know that I am God. Be still, and know. Be still. Be.
What have your hands been gripping, as if the earth might give way without you? Could you let it rest in His, for the length of one breath? Breathe in... God is our refuge. Breathe out... I can let go.
Let's pray. God, our refuge and strength, so much is shaking, and I cannot steady it all. Be in the midst of me. Be in the midst of the people and places I am afraid for. Help me to let go, and to know that You are God. Amen.
When the waters get loud again, remember the river. And remember how the psalm ends... the Lord of hosts is with us. Let that be your place to pause. Go in peace.
