Welcome to Peace in the Psalms. This reading is called I Lift My Eyes. We'll listen to Psalm one hundred twenty-one. It is a short psalm, made for people on the way... so you can come to it in the middle of things.
Wherever you are, let your eyes soften. When we worry, our looking grows narrow. We stare at the screen, the floor, the problem just in front of us. If you can, look up for a moment... to a window, a ceiling, a piece of sky. Or simply lift your attention.
This is one of the Songs of Ascents, psalms that travelers may have sung as they climbed toward Jerusalem. The road was long, with loose stones underfoot, hot sun, and hills where danger could hide. It begins with an honest question... where will my help come from?
I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help. My help cometh from the Lord, which made heaven and earth.
He will not suffer thy foot to be moved: he that keepeth thee will not slumber. Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep. The Lord is thy keeper: the Lord is thy shade upon thy right hand. The sun shall not smite thee by day, nor the moon by night.
The Lord shall preserve thee from all evil: he shall preserve thy soul. The Lord shall preserve thy going out and thy coming in from this time forth, and even for evermore.
My help cometh from the Lord, which made heaven and earth. The traveler looks at the hills, and then past them, to the One who made the hills. What is in front of you may be very large. It is not larger than its Maker.
And did you hear how often this psalm says that you are kept? He keepeth. Thy keeper. He shall preserve. In the Hebrew, it is the same word again and again, six times in eight short verses. As though the singer knew that a worried heart needs to be told more than once.
So let yourself be told again. Your foot is kept, on uncertain ground. Your days are kept, under a hard sun. Your nights are kept, under the moon. Your going out is kept. Your coming in is kept. Your soul is kept.
He does not slumber. Think of what that means for you. You don't have to be the one who watches every hour. You can close your eyes, or turn your back on the problem for a while... and nothing you love will be left without a keeper.
Being kept doesn't mean the road has no stones. You may still stumble, and some days will hurt. It means you are never unattended on it. From this time forth, the psalm says... which means beginning now, right where you are standing.
Is there a going out ahead of you, something you must walk into? Or a person whose coming and going you worry over? Picture that doorway now. And picture the Lord already standing in it... keeping the going out, and the coming in.
Let's pray. Maker of heaven and earth, lift my eyes from what frightens me to the One who helps me. Watch over my steps today, and over the ones I love where I cannot follow them. When I am too tired to keep watch, thank You that You never are. Amen.
The next time you pass through a door, let it remind you. Going out, and coming in... I am kept. Lift your eyes, and go gently.
