I'm Sarah Mitchell.
Tonight we're asking a question: how completely does Jesus save you?
The writer of Hebrews gives us an answer that reaches all the way to this very moment, as you lie here in the quiet.
Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.
Three words stand at the heart of this promise: to the uttermost.
The phrase means completely, fully, forever.
Jesus saves you not partially, not temporarily, but all the way through.
This isn't a salvation that covers only part of your life or part of your need.
It reaches from the moment you first believed to this night, and onward to the last day of your life and beyond.
Now notice what makes this complete salvation possible.
The verse says he ever lives to make intercession.
Jesus is not a savior who did his work and walked away.
He lives right now, and his life has a purpose: he is praying for you.
In the ancient temple, a high priest would enter the holy place to intercede for the people, but his work was never finished.
He would have to return again and again, year after year.
But Jesus entered once, and he remains there, alive and interceding without end.
This means that at this very hour, Jesus is presenting you before the Father.
He is speaking on your behalf, holding up the worth of his own sacrifice as the reason you are accepted.
Your standing before God does not rest on how well you prayed today or how strong your faith felt.
It rests on him, on his unending life and unceasing prayer.
Think of the ones who come to God by him.
That phrase describes every believer.
If you have come to the Father through Jesus, this promise is yours.
You did not come because you were strong or worthy.
You came because he made a way, and he keeps that way open every moment.
The word intercession means to plead for another, to stand in the gap.
When your own prayers feel weak or wordless, he is praying.
When you are too tired to form a thought, he is interceding.
When guilt whispers that you have failed too many times, his voice before the Father is stronger than any accusation.
Earlier in this same letter, the writer describes Jesus as a high priest who is touched with the feeling of our infirmities.
For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.
He knows what it is to be human.
He knows weariness and sorrow and the weight of a long day.
And so his prayers for you are not distant or abstract.
They are informed by true compassion.
This is grace that does not run out.
You cannot exhaust the intercession of Jesus by needing it too often.
You cannot wear out his patience or his willingness to present you as his own.
The apostle Paul, writing to the Romans, asked who could bring a charge against God's elect.
Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God’s elect?
It is God that justifieth.
Who is he that condemneth?
It is Christ that died, yea rather, that is risen again, who is even at the right hand of God, who also maketh intercession for us.
The answer is no one.
No accusation can stand, because Christ has died and risen and now sits at the right hand of God, interceding.
So what does this mean for you tonight, as you let go of the day?
It means you can rest in a salvation that does not depend on you holding on tightly.
Jesus holds you.
It means that the mistakes you made today, the words you wish you could take back, the patience you lost, none of it has moved you outside his care.
He is praying for you still.
It means you are not saved only up to a point, only as far as your strength could carry you.
You are saved to the uttermost, completely, held by a savior who will never stop interceding.
You do not have to lie here wondering if you have done enough or been enough.
The question has already been answered by the one who gave himself for you and who lives to present you before the Father.
Let that truth settle over you now.
Jesus is praying for you tonight, and his prayers are heard.
Let us pray.
Father, thank you that Jesus ever lives to intercede for us, and that his salvation reaches all the way to this quiet moment.
Help us rest in the completeness of what he has done.
Goodnight.
