Tonight, we're asking how one event... the death of Jesus... can be described in such different ways.
Scripture speaks of ransom and sacrifice, redemption and propitiation, as though the cross accomplished many things at once.
We're going to look at two pictures the Bible gives us... ransom, and justice... and see how they both point to the same love.
Mark records Jesus saying something striking about his mission.
For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister... and to give his life a ransom for many.
A ransom is a price paid to set captives free.
In the ancient world, prisoners of war or kidnap victims could be released when the price their captors demanded was paid.
Jesus uses this image to describe his death.
He came to give his life as the payment that would release many from captivity.
But captivity to what?
Not to human enemies... but to sin, and its consequence, which is death.
We were held by a debt we could never pay, under a sentence we could never lift.
Jesus gave his life to pay what we owed... and set us free.
Now, listen to how Paul describes the same event, using the language of justice.
For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God; being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.
Paul says we are justified freely by grace, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.
Redemption is ransom language... the buying back of something lost.
But Paul doesn't stop there.
Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God; to declare, I say, at this time his righteousness... that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus.
Here Paul introduces the idea of propitiation... a word that means the turning away of wrath through a sacrifice.
God set forth Jesus to demonstrate his righteousness... his justice.
In his forbearance, God had passed over sins committed before the cross, waiting for the full and final answer.
And when Christ died, God's justice was declared.
He could be both just... and the one who justifies anyone who has faith.
So we have two pictures... ransom and justice... mercy and righteousness.
How do they fit together?
The cross is a ransom, because it sets us free from captivity to sin and death.
Jesus gave his life as the price, and we who believe are released.
But the cross is also the satisfaction of divine justice.
Sin is not overlooked, or swept aside.
God's righteousness is upheld... declared... made visible for all creation to see.
The same blood that ransoms us... also satisfies the righteous requirements of God's law.
Think of it this way.
A ransom answers the question... what will it cost to set them free?
Justice answers the question... how can guilt be dealt with, and righteousness maintained?
Both questions find their answer in the death of Jesus.
He paid the price... and bore the penalty.
He released the captives... and vindicated the Judge.
This is why the New Testament writers use so many different images.
No single picture can contain the fullness of what happened on the cross.
Ransom, sacrifice, propitiation, reconciliation, redemption.
Each one opens a window onto the same astounding truth... God has made a way for sinners to be saved.
And because the cross is both ransom and justice, we can rest tonight in two great certainties.
First... you are truly free.
If you have faith in Jesus, the ransom has been paid in full.
You are no longer held captive by sin, or under the shadow of death.
The price was his life... and the receipt is his resurrection.
Second... you stand on solid ground.
Your forgiveness is not a legal fiction, or a kindly overlooking of guilt.
God has not bent the rules, or ignored his own righteousness.
He has satisfied every requirement of justice in the death of his Son.
You are forgiven... because the debt has been paid, and the penalty has been borne.

The cross was enough.

Ransom and justice, mercy and righteousness... all meet in the love of God.
